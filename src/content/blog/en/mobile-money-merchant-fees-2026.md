A shop in Nairobi takes KSh 1,200 on the till. The collection fee is about KSh 6.60. Then the owner moves that money to a personal wallet to pay a supplier, and that single transfer costs KSh 12.

**The payout cost more than the sale did.** That inversion is the part most fee comparisons miss, and it is where a trading month quietly loses money.

## You pay three times, not once

Mobile money charges a shop at three distinct points: collecting the payment, moving the balance out of the till, and turning it into cash or bank money. Published rate cards almost always describe the first one.

The three are priced differently, which matters more than the headline percentage. Collection is a percentage per sale, so it scales with turnover and cannot be optimised. Payouts are banded or capped, so they reward batching. Withdrawals are flat per slice of cash, so they punish anyone using an agent as a bank.

## Kenya: collection got cheaper on 7 August 2026

Safaricom revised its merchant tariffs on 7 August 2026, in a move led by the Central Bank of Kenya ([The Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/business/article/2001554316/safaricom-halves-m-pesa-merchant-fees-in-cbk-led-move)).

| Item | Before | From 7 August 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buy Goods free band | up to KSh 200 | up to KSh 500 |
| Buy Goods above the band | 0.55 %, capped at KSh 200 | unchanged |
| Till to wallet, KSh 1,001 to 1,500 | KSh 23 | KSh 12 |
| Till to wallet, KSh 45,001 and above | KSh 108 | KSh 54 |
| Till to paybill, KSh 1,001 to 1,500 | KSh 15 | KSh 8 |

The payout cuts reach 50 per cent, which is the larger change for a shop that moves money daily ([Business Today](https://businesstoday.co.ke/safaricom-makes-big-cuts-on-m-pesa-merchant-charges-from-this-august/)).

One thing to verify on your own statement rather than from press coverage: whether the 0.55 per cent sits with your customer or with you. Reporting describes the free band as a customer benefit, and merchant agreements differ. Read one month of settlement lines before you reprice anything.

Micro-traders have a deadline this week. The Pochi la Biashara promotion that waives charges on customer payments up to KSh 200 runs from 1 August to **31 October 2026**, after which the tiered fees resume ([The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/safaricom-reduces-m-pesa-business-charges-list-of-new-charges/)).

## Ghana: the tax left, the bank charge stayed

Ghana's electronic transfer levy was repealed on 2 April 2025, and claims that it has returned are false ([GhanaFact](https://ghanafact.com/fact-check-angel-fm-presenters-claim-that-gra-reintroduced-e-levy-is-false/)).

What traders still pay is a commercial charge that arrived in its place in public attention: MTN applies **0.75 per cent, capped at GH¢ 5**, on transfers from a MoMo wallet to a bank account ([Yen](https://yen.com.gh/people/305233-concerns-ghanaians-mtn-imposes-075-charges-transfers-momo-wallets-bank-accounts/)).

The cap is the whole story for a merchant. A GH¢ 700 transfer costs GH¢ 5.25 before the cap bites, and so does a GH¢ 7,000 transfer. Banking once a week instead of every evening cuts that line by roughly 85 per cent, and the money is safer in the account than in a wallet a staff member can reach.

Merchant collection rates in Ghana are set contract by contract, and the figures circulating on comparison blogs are estimates rather than published tariffs. Ask your aggregator for the rate in writing.

## Nigeria: the N50 moved to the sender

From 1 January 2026, the N50 electronic money transfer levy became stamp duty under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, and banks now debit the sender instead of the recipient ([TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/stamp-duty-replaces-emtl-sender-to-bear-50-charge-on-bank-transfers)).

Three details decide what it costs your shop:

- It applies to transfers of **N10,000 and above**. Transfers below that, salary payments and intra-bank transfers are reported as exempt ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/?p=529828)).
- Your provider's own transfer fee sits on top. Paystack, for example, charges tiers of N10, N25 and N50 by amount band, plus the N50 duty ([Paystack](https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/7573314)).
- Cash withdrawal through an agent terminal is set at **N100 per N20,000** under the Central Bank's fee guide, against informal pricing that previously reached N100 per N5,000 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/04/27/why-transfers-above-%e2%82%a610000-will-cost-%e2%82%a660/)).

The practical effect is that a customer paying N12,000 by transfer now feels N60 leave their account, and some will ask you to absorb it. Since the duty is per transaction and not a percentage, basket size decides the damage: ten sales of N10,000 carry N500 of duty, while one sale of N100,000 carries N50.

## What a day of takings actually costs

Take the local equivalent of 100,000 in daily takings, collected and then moved out once.

| Market | Collection | One payout | Total cost of the day |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kenya, 100 sales of KSh 1,000 | KSh 550 (0.55 % each) | KSh 54 to a wallet | KSh 604, or 0.60 % |
| Kenya, 200 sales of KSh 500 | KSh 0 (inside the free band) | KSh 54 | KSh 54, or 0.05 % |
| Ghana, wallet to bank | contract rate, confirm in writing | GH¢ 5 (capped) | cap reached above GH¢ 667 |
| Nigeria, 10 transfers of N10,000 | N500 duty plus provider tiers | intra-bank move exempt | from N500, or 0.50 % |

The second row is the one to read twice. The same turnover, split into smaller tickets, collects for nothing in Kenya because every sale falls inside the free band. Basket size is now a fee variable, which is a strange thing to say about retail pricing and a real one.

## Three habits that cut the bill

**Batch the payouts.** This is the only lever with a guaranteed return, because caps and bands mean one large transfer costs a fraction of several small ones. Set a fixed time of day, move the balance once, and record it.

**Stop cashing out money you plan to bank.** Withdrawing at an agent and depositing at a branch pays two fees to end up where a single transfer would have put you. Agent float has its own economics, which we covered in [agent banking or merchant payments](/en/blog/agent-banking-vs-merchant-payments/).

**Never surcharge the customer.** A visible mobile money fee at the counter pushes customers to accept a staff member's personal number, which looks like a completed sale and leaves no record in your books. You save 0.55 per cent and lose the sale.

## What your POS has to show you

A fee schedule is useless if your records cannot be matched against the provider statement. Three requirements, in order.

Payment method must be recorded per sale, so the till total splits into cash, mobile money and card without arithmetic. The payout and the collection must be visible as separate lines, because they are separate costs with separate remedies. And each branch or counter needs its own till number, or a shortfall cannot be placed.
