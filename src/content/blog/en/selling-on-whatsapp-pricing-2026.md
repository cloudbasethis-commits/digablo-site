A shop in Nairobi that answers 150 WhatsApp messages a day had no messaging bill in September. In October it has one. Meta started charging for business replies on 1 October 2026, and the change lands hardest on the exact businesses that built their counter around chat.

Reach explains why this matters here more than in Europe. Industry roundups put WhatsApp use at roughly 97 percent of internet users in Kenya and 95 percent in Nigeria, where one [recent study](https://brandspurng.com/2026/09/26/whatsapp-installed-on-95-of-nigerian-smartphones-study-finds/) found the app installed on 95 percent of smartphones. A 2025 survey of self-employed adults in Kenya, Nigeria, India and Pakistan found **58 percent of Nigerian women and 65 percent of Nigerian men** using WhatsApp to run livelihood activities, from product photos to order follow up ([TechTrendsKE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/05/16/whatsapp-african-women-business-livelihoods/)).

For a lot of shops, WhatsApp is not marketing. It is the till queue.

## What changed on 1 October 2026

Replies you send inside the 24 hour customer service window, previously free, are now billed after a monthly allowance. Each business phone number gets **1,000 free service messages per month**, and billing starts at message 1,001.

Three details decide how much this costs you.

Utility templates sent as replies inside an open window were free from July 2025. They are now charged with no free tier at all. Incoming customer messages stay free, so a busy inbox costs nothing until you answer. And the 72 hour free entry point window that applies to conversations started from Click to WhatsApp ads is unchanged, which quietly makes paid ads the cheapest way to open a conversation.

Rates are set by the recipient's country code, not by where your business sits. A Nairobi shop answering a Nigerian customer pays the Nigerian rate.

## If you answer by hand on the free app, nothing changed

The WhatsApp Business app remains free to download and use, with no per-message charges. If one person replies from one phone, the October change does not reach you.

The billing applies to the WhatsApp Business Platform, the API that chatbots, shared inboxes, CRMs and automated order confirmations run through. Most shops reach it through a Business Solution Provider, which adds its own markup on top of Meta's rate, commonly estimated between 0.003 and 0.010 US dollars per message.

That distinction matters before you shop for automation. The free app's real cost is staff time, roughly a minute per conversation that nobody logs anywhere. The API's cost is now visible on an invoice. Neither is free.

## What a reply costs in your market

Published rate cards for 1 October 2026 put African markets in three bands. Figures are US dollars per delivered message.

| Market | Marketing | Utility | Service |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rest of Africa, including Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda | 0.0225 | 0.0040 | 0.0040 |
| Nigeria | 0.0516 | 0.0067 | 0.0067 |
| South Africa | 0.0379 | 0.0095 | 0.0095 |

Sources agree on the shape and disagree on the decimals. [SleekFlow](https://sleekflow.io/blog/whatsapp-business-price) and Zernio list Nigeria marketing at 0.0516, while ManyChat's October table shows 0.0593 and a utility rate of 0.0077. Nigerian press reports in August cited about 0.0101, roughly 14 naira, for the service and utility rate, a figure [FCB.ai](https://fcb.ai/articles/whatsapp-pricing-october-2026-cost-per-resolution) notes does not appear on Meta's published card. Kenyan outlets quoted 0.0040 as about 52 cents of a shilling per message.

Two adjustments to make before you trust a number. South African rates are quoted excluding 15 percent VAT, according to [ChatMaxima](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/south-africa/). And utility and authentication templates carry volume discounts at higher monthly tiers, while service messages do not, so support traffic never gets cheaper per unit.

## Run the maths on your own volume

A mid-sized shop fielding 40 chat conversations a day, with four outbound replies each, sends about 4,800 billable-category messages a month. Subtract the 1,000 free service messages and 3,800 remain.

| Market | Monthly cost at 3,800 billed service replies | With a 0.005 provider markup |
|---|---|---|
| Kenya, at 0.0040 | about 15 USD | about 34 USD |
| Nigeria, at 0.0067 | about 25 USD | about 44 USD |
| South Africa, at 0.0095, before VAT | about 36 USD | about 55 USD |

Those are not frightening numbers for a shop turning over thousands of dollars a month. They become one in two cases: a business running several numbers for several branches, since the 1,000 free messages are per number rather than per company, and a business that sends broadcast marketing, where Nigeria's 0.0516 per message turns a 5,000 contact campaign into roughly 258 dollars.

## The expensive part was never the messages

A WhatsApp conversation produces an agreement, not a record. The order does not reduce stock, does not generate a receipt and carries no payment reference, which is why shops selling through chat find their books and their shelves disagreeing by month end.

Three failures show up repeatedly. The item is promised to one customer in chat and sold to another at the counter, because nothing reserved it. The customer sends a screenshot of a mobile money confirmation and the goods go out, which is the oldest fraud in the book and one we cover in our [M-Pesa merchant guide](/en/blog/m-pesa-pos-guide/). And the staff member handling the phone becomes the only person who knows what was agreed, a single point of failure that also enables the leakage described in our piece on [theft at the till](/en/blog/till-fraud-stock-loss-controls/).

A chat order that is keyed into the point of sale system becomes a normal sale: stock moves, a receipt exists, the mobile money reference sits on the transaction. That one habit is worth more than every message you might save.

## Five ways to cut the bill without losing the customer

**Close the conversation inside the window.** A question answered within 24 hours uses your service allowance. The same question answered three days later needs a paid template, and marketing rates run four to eight times the service rate.

**Consolidate your numbers.** Three branch numbers give you 3,000 free service messages but triple the provider fees and split your customer history. One number with clear routing usually wins.

**Put the price list in the catalogue.** Most chat volume is price checks. A current catalogue and a pinned price list remove several round trips per order.

**Use Click to WhatsApp ads deliberately.** The 72 hour free entry point window survives the October change, so a conversation that starts from an ad is cheaper to serve than one you reopen with a template.

**Automate confirmations, not conversations.** A single order confirmation with the amount, the reference and the collection time prevents three follow up questions. Automating small talk just multiplies billed messages.

## What to ask before you connect anything

Four questions sort a useful setup from an expensive one. Does the chat order land in the point of sale system, or only in an inbox? Does stock move when the order is confirmed? Is the mobile money reference stored on the sale and printed on the receipt? And does any of it keep working when the connection drops, which in practice it will.
