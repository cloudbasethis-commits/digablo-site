South Africa has gone 518 consecutive days without load shedding, counted from 16 May 2025 ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/eskoms-turnaround-gains-momentum-as-weekly-eaf-reaches-72-56-outage-levels-decline-sharply-and-load-reduction-impact-falls-to-6-8-as-seven-provinces-are-removed-from-the-load-reduction-schedule/)). In Kenya, over the same period, the average power interruption got longer: three hours and 14 minutes in the year to June 2026, against two hours and 34 minutes a year earlier, a jump of roughly 26 per cent ([The Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/national/article/2001559722/kenyans-spend-longer-in-the-dark-as-power-reliability-drops), citing Kenya Power and EPRA).

Two grids, opposite directions, one conclusion for a shopkeeper. Your ability to sell should not track your utility's news cycle.

## The number that sets your budget is watts, not kilowatts

The single decision that moves the cost of backup power is which device runs your till.

A phone or tablet draws 10 to 20 watts on its charger nameplate. A desktop till is a different animal: the PC and monitor sit around 120 to 200 watts, the thermal receipt printer's power brick is commonly rated 24 V at 2.5 A, so about 60 watts at peak, and the router adds another 10. Put together, the counter needs ten to fifteen times more power to do the same job.

That ratio is the whole article. **A shop that sells from a tablet needs a power bank. The same shop on a desktop till needs an inverter and a battery.** Nobody selling you hardware will frame it that way, because the second answer is worth twenty times more in margin.

## What outages look like in 2026, country by country

The risk is not uniform across the markets we work in, so copying a neighbour's setup is a poor method.

**Kenya.** Beyond the longer average interruption, there was a nationwide blackout on the evening of 29 July 2026, with supply restored by 2am and no specific fault named. Planned maintenance is the bigger operational problem: Kenya Power announced interruptions across 13 counties for 21 October 2026, and earlier notices described cuts of eight to nine hours.

**Nigeria.** The national grid has collapsed more than 160 times since privatisation in 2013 ([ICIR](https://www.icirnigeria.org/nigerians-face-more-grid-collapses-as-nerc-reports-unresolved-weaknesses/)). Band A customers pay roughly ₦209 per kWh for a promised minimum of 20 hours a day, and regulators have ordered distribution companies to compensate Band A customers who did not get those hours in the first quarter of 2026 ([The Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/featured/less-for-more-how-erratic-supply-shortchanges-band-a-households-industries/)). Paying the premium tariff does not buy you out of the problem.

**Ghana.** ECG ruled out a load-shedding timetable in April 2026, attributing outages to repairs after a fire at GRIDCo's Akosombo substation, while still running planned maintenance cuts from 9am to 4pm in the Tema, Ashanti and Western regions. No timetable is harder to plan around than a bad timetable.

**South Africa.** Load shedding is on hold, but load reduction still affects about 193,556 customers, with Eskom targeting March 2027 to end it. Eskom's own diesel bill fell by R4.79 billion, or 80.49 per cent, between 1 April and 1 October 2026, which is the clearest measure of how much the picture has changed there.

Read the first and third rows together. For a shop that sells from a tablet with offline software, the cost of never losing a sale to a blackout is under five thousand shillings. For the identical shop on a desktop till, it is over four hundred thousand naira. Same turnover, same outages, two budgets with no overlap.

## Fuel is the line that quietly doubles the bill

A generator's purchase price is the small number. In mid-October 2026, NNPC sold petrol at ₦1,355 a litre in Lagos while most independent stations sat between ₦1,380 and ₦1,400 ([Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/10/petrol-marketers-hike-pump-price-in-abuja-lagos-by-n29-litre/)), and NNPC's ₦66 discount was extended only to 31 October 2026. Depot diesel was near ₦1,790 a litre, roughly a third more.

Reckon on 0.7 litres an hour for a small set, which is the order of magnitude its nameplate suggests. Eight hours of outage burns about 5.6 litres, so **₦7,600 per cut at the NNPC price**. Two cuts a week comes to ₦790,000 a year in fuel alone, before servicing, before the two-hour queue, before the night someone siphons the tank.

At that run rate, the inverter and battery in row three pay for themselves inside seven months, and they do it without noise or carbon monoxide at the back door. The generator still earns its place if you have a freezer. It is a poor way to power a till.

## Offline software is half of the power plan

Buying a battery for a till that stops working without the internet solves nothing. You have to power the router, the modem, the ONT and whatever tower-side link sits behind them, and you still lose sales when the outage took out the base station rather than your shop.

Software that records sales locally and syncs later cuts the load you must back up to a single device. That is the argument we make at length in our guide to [offline POS software](/en/blog/offline-pos-software/), and it is worth checking the detail: some vendors call a read-only product list an offline mode. The question to ask is whether a complete sale, including payment method and stock movement, can be recorded and later reconciled with no connection at all.

## The risks nobody puts in the outage budget

A dark shop is not merely a slower shop.

Stock walks. Our piece on [till fraud and stock loss controls](/en/blog/till-fraud-stock-loss-controls/) makes the point that most shrinkage happens where supervision is thin, and an outage removes supervision from the whole floor at once.

Payment verification collapses. Mobile money confirmations are the standard target: with the counter screen dark, staff accept the customer's screenshot because they have nothing else to look at. Keep the rule absolute, outage or not. No credit notification on your own device, no goods out.

Reopening is when the errors land. When power returns, sales recorded on paper have to go into the till, syncs have to complete, and the drawer has to be counted against both. Do it the same evening, never the next morning.

## A 30-minute drill worth running once

Cut the power to your counter deliberately, on a quiet afternoon, and time yourself. Can you take a full sale within 60 seconds? Does the last sale before the cut still exist? Does the till reconnect and sync without someone logging in again? Can you issue any kind of receipt?

Most shops discover two things in that half hour. The backup they own is smaller than they thought, and the software is the part that actually failed.
