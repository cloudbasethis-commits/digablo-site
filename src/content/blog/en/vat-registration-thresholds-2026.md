A shop turning over the equivalent of 40,000 US dollars a year sits comfortably below the VAT line in South Africa, is squarely above it in Ghana if it sells services, and sits in a grey zone in Nigeria that three different tax advisers will describe three different ways.

Four of the five markets below moved their thresholds in 2026. Two moved up, one split in two, and one tax authority proposed removing its threshold entirely. If you are near the line, the number matters less than what happens to your till the day you cross it.

## Where the lines sit in 2026

Here are the current figures, with the test period each authority applies.

| Market | Compulsory registration | Voluntary from | Standard rate | Test period |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| South Africa | R2.3m (from 1 April 2026, was R1m) | R120,000 (was R50,000) | 15% | Any 12 consecutive months |
| Kenya | KSh 5m | Below KSh 5m | 16% | Rolling year |
| Ghana | GHS 750,000 for goods (was GHS 200,000); no threshold for services | Commissioner-General's discretion | 15% plus 5% levies | Annual turnover |
| Nigeria | Reported as N25m, N50m, or no minimum | N/A | 7.5% | Any 12 consecutive months |
| United Kingdom | £90,000 | Below £90,000 | 20% | Any rolling 12 months |

Two patterns stand out. The thresholds that moved, moved a long way: South Africa's compulsory line more than doubled, Ghana's goods threshold nearly quadrupled. And the test is almost never the calendar year, which is the mistake that catches most owners out.

## South Africa: the threshold doubled, but nothing happens automatically

Registration is now compulsory at R2.3 million, up from R1 million, with effect from 1 April 2026, and the voluntary floor rose from R50,000 to R120,000 ([Standard Bank](https://www.standardbank.co.za/southafrica/news-and-media/newsroom/budget-2026-delivers-sme-relief-as-vat-threshold-increases-to-r2.3-million)).

The detail that costs money: a business already registered and now below the new line does not drop out by itself. It has to apply to deregister, and it keeps filing returns until SARS confirms the cancellation. Miss a return during that window and you collect penalties on a registration you were trying to leave.

Worth checking before you rush. Deregistering means you stop claiming input VAT on stock, rent and equipment. For a retailer with real purchase volumes, staying registered can be worth more than the admin it costs, which is why advisers are treating this as a calculation rather than an automatic exit ([Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2026/South-Africa/Tax-Exchange-Control/tax-and-exchange-control-alert-19-march-VAT-threshold-increased-Should-SMEs-remain-registered)).

The qualifying turnover for Turnover Tax rose to R2.3 million on the same date, so the two regimes still line up.

## Ghana: goods got relief, services got the opposite

The Value Added Tax Act, 2025 (Act 1151) took effect on 1 January 2026. The registration threshold for traders in goods rose from GHS 200,000 to GHS 750,000. For services, the turnover threshold was abolished, which means registration is mandatory unless the Commissioner-General permits otherwise ([Crowe Ghana](https://www.crowe.com/gh/news/ghana-vat-reform-2026)).

So a Ghanaian hairdresser, mechanic or consultant now registers from the first cedi of turnover, while a shop selling the same value of goods does not register until GHS 750,000. If you do both, which many small businesses do, the services side pulls you in.

Two other changes matter at the counter. The VAT Flat Rate Scheme is gone, so retailers who used it now operate on the standard regime with input credits. And the NHIL and GETFund levies, 2.5% each on top of the 15% VAT, became deductible as input tax, which changes the arithmetic on every purchase invoice you file.

## Kenya: KSh 5 million, and eTIMS regardless

Registration is mandatory once taxable supplies reach, or can reasonably be expected to reach, KSh 5 million in a year. The forward-looking half of that test is the one traders forget: you register when you can see the line coming, not after you cross it.

Two things to watch. KRA has proposed scrapping the threshold altogether so that every trader, including small-scale ones, registers and charges 16% VAT ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/kra-prepares-16pc-vat-shocker-on-small-traders-5399650)). As of mid 2026 that remained a proposal, not law. You should also discount the KSh 8 million figure that circulates in some guides, which the official KRA position does not support.

More immediately relevant: **eTIMS already applies whether you are VAT registered or not**. Under the Tax Procedures (Electronic Tax Invoice) Regulations 2024, every business in Kenya must generate and transmit invoices electronically, and a non-registered business still needs electronic invoices for its expenses to be income tax deductible. The threshold decides whether you charge VAT. It does not decide whether you issue compliant invoices.

## Nigeria: the threshold nobody agrees on

Be honest with yourself about this one: published 2026 guidance does not converge. Some advisers report mandatory VAT registration above 50 million naira of turnover in any 12 consecutive months, others say the old 25 million naira line still applies, and others state that the Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025 sets no turnover-only minimum at all.

What is better established is the small business relief. A business with turnover of 100 million naira or less and total fixed assets of 250 million naira or less, and which does not provide professional services, falls inside the small company test and is relieved of charging VAT and filing monthly returns.

Practical advice, given the fog: get your position in writing from the Nigeria Revenue Service or a licensed practitioner, and keep your records as though you were registered. Reconstructing a year of line-item sales after a ruling goes against you costs far more than capturing it as you go.

## The UK: £90,000, and the trap underneath it

The threshold is £90,000 of taxable turnover in any rolling 12 month period, unchanged since April 2024, with deregistration available at £88,000. A September 2025 Treasury answer in the House of Lords confirmed the figure and noted it is higher than any EU country's.

The trap is behavioural. At 20% VAT, crossing £90,000 means either raising prices by a fifth or absorbing the rate out of margin, which pushes a lot of small traders into deliberately capping turnover just under the line. If you are approaching it, model both scenarios on real numbers before you start turning work away.

## What crossing the line does to your till

More than you would expect, and almost none of it is about the return you file.

- **Per-line calculation.** A tax invoice has to show the VAT amount and the rate. Your system has to compute it per item, not as a rounded figure at the bottom, because a buyer claiming input VAT needs a document that reconciles line by line.
- **Inclusive or exclusive pricing.** Decide once, configure it once. Shops that switch halfway through a year end up with two incompatible sets of sales history.
- **The registration number on every receipt.** Omit it and the invoice is not a valid tax invoice, whatever the total says.
- **Supplier invoices you can actually use.** Input VAT only offsets output VAT if you hold a compliant invoice for it. In Nigeria, an invoice that never passed through the national platform gets no invoice reference number, which means your buyer cannot claim input VAT on it either ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/04/16/all-you-need-to-know-about-e-invoicing-in-nigeria/)).
- **Separate rates in one basket.** Exempt and zero-rated items sitting alongside standard-rated ones is normal in grocery. A till that cannot hold different rates per product will misstate your return every month.

If you run more than one location, the threshold is usually tested on the business, not the branch, so combined turnover is what counts. ## Four things to do before you cross

1. **Measure on a rolling 12 months, today.** Not this calendar year. Add the last 12 months of sales and compare against your market's line.
2. **Project forward six months.** Kenya and the UK both oblige you to register on reasonable expectation, not only on fact.
3. **Price the decision, do not assume it.** Compute your input VAT on stock, rent and equipment against the output VAT you would charge. Registration sometimes pays.
4. **Check your till can issue a tax invoice** with per-line VAT, your registration number and multiple rates, before you need it.
